🔒 Privacy Center

Actionable security checklists and guides for smartphone permissions, two-factor authentication, and safe evidence preservation.

Many social and utility apps track your location continuously in the background, making it possible for stalkers or data brokers to map your daily routine.

  • On iOS (iPhone): Open Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services. Set social apps (Instagram, Snapchat, X, dating apps) to "Never" or "While Using App". Ensure "Precise Location" is toggled OFF for non-navigation apps.
  • On Android: Open Settings → Location → App Permissions. Audit apps listed under "Allowed all the time" and change them to "Allow only while using the app" or "Don't allow".
  • Disable "Significant Locations": Both operating systems quietly log frequent places (home, college, work). Turn off Significant Locations under System Services to protect your schedule.

If someone creates a fake Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn profile using your photos and name, take these specific steps:

  1. Do not immediately message the fake profile: The perpetrator may quickly change the handle or delete traces before you gather proof.
  2. Copy the exact profile URL: Write down the exact alphanumeric username and profile link (e.g. instagram.com/fake_handle).
  3. File an Impersonation Report on the platform: Use the dedicated "They are pretending to be me" option. Most platforms prioritize reports filed by the actual victim.
  4. Preserve screenshots: Document the profile bio, profile picture, and any malicious stories or posts before they are deleted.

Many account takeovers happen because people reused the same password on several sites.When one site is breached,attackers try the leaked password on other accounts.

  • The Passphrase Rule: Instead of hard-to-remember strings like P@ssw0rd!, use 4-5 unrelated words: sunset-lavender-coffee-bicycle-jump. It is exponentially harder for brute-force tools to crack and easy for you to remember.
  • Use a Free Password Manager: Tools like Bitwarden or Apple Keychain generate unique 16+ character passwords for every website, so a breach on one site never compromises your email or social media.
  • Never share credentials over chat: Legitimate college faculty, bank officers, or app administrators will never ask for your password or PIN.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) ensures that even if someone learns your password, they cannot log into your account without a second verification factor.

  • The Risk of SMS 2FA: SMS verification codes are vulnerable to SIM-swapping, cellular interception, and lock-screen notification preview leaks.
  • Use Authenticator Apps: Download Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator. They generate time-based one-time passwords (TOTP) directly on your device without transmitting over cellular networks.
  • Save Backup Recovery Codes: When setting up 2FA, copy your 8-digit emergency backup codes and store them in a secure, offline location.

While your instinct may be to block a harasser immediately, doing so before capturing proper evidence can prevent police or college disciplinary committees from taking action.

  1. Take full-screen screenshots: Do not crop the images. The phone's status bar (showing date, time, battery, and network carrier) establishes crucial chronological proof.
  2. Capture sender identification: Tap the sender's profile to screenshot their exact username, phone number, and bio.
  3. Export WhatsApp / Telegram chat transcripts: Use Export Chat → Without Media to save a timestamped text file of the full conversation.
  4. Do not alter or edit images: Keep original image files intact without filters or markups to preserve metadata for legal admissibility.

Under the UGC Regulations and the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act, every university and college must maintain an active Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) with 50% women representation to handle student grievances confidentially.

About NexaSafe

NexaSafe is a women's safety and digital empowerment platform built for college students. It brings emergency contacts, safe-journey check-ins, a message scanner, private incident records and privacy guides into one place. Your incident records stay in your own browser and are never uploaded.

Important: This is a demo project. It does not send real SOS alerts. In an emergency, call 112 or 1091 directly.
